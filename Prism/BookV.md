# V. Setting and Anthropy

The Realms of Prism are seven. The world is one. It was not always so, and the distance between those two sentences is the whole history of the world.

In the time before, there was one world and one light upon it. Seven kingdoms shared that world, and each kingdom was a school of magic and a branch of wisdom — the seat of one color of the Prism, where its arts were taught and its philosophy argued. Magic flowed freely between them. The elements intertwined. It was an age of balance, and it lasted long enough that those who lived in it believed it would last forever.

It ended with the Lonely Muse.

She was not a tyrant, and she was never a conqueror. She was the most gifted student the seven kingdoms ever produced, and she was relentlessly curious: a young woman who wanted to understand everything the light could be refracted into, and who walked from kingdom to kingdom to learn it. At every school she was welcomed for her talent, and from every school she was eventually cast out. She saw too clearly. She mended what she found broken, and the people whose comfort rested on the broken things could not believe that her kindness had no angle, so they invented one and sent her away. She left each gate more alone than she had arrived at it, and she kept walking. The last school she mastered was Light.

A mortal who holds all seven schools holds what the Titans hold. That is what she became — not what she sought, but what her seeking made of her. The Titans, led by the wisdom of the First Titan, judged that the world could not bear a second power of their own kind within it, whatever that power intended. They did not kill her. They shattered the Prism.

The full telling of her story, and of the ages before it, is Book VI. This book describes the world the Shattering left behind.

---

## The Shattering and the Shape of the World

What the Titans broke was the unity of the world, not its ground. The seven kingdoms were torn loose from the world they had stood upon and set into the heavens, each as a wandering body in the sky, each still the seat of its color and of its Titan. These are the Realms of Prism as they hang today: seven lights in the night, one for every school of magic. What remained below — the land, the seas, and the people on them — is the one world that mortals live on now. Its name is **Home**.

Nothing of the magic was lost. The seven realms shine down upon Home, and their light refracts into the world as it always did. A mage on Home still draws on Red when Fire rides high, still reads Prophecy in the station of Light. What changed is that the sources are no longer here. The schools endure as institutions on Home, each teaching the color its realm pours down; the realms themselves stand apart in the sky. Between Home and the realms the Titans built doors, and then they stood in them.

Mortals have had a hundred thousand years to study the new shape of things, and they draw it as the **septisigil** (`septisigil.svg`): a seven-pointed figure whose inner lines show how the colors descend from one another, and whose outer ring shows how they combine. The descent is the order of creation itself, as Book VI tells it.

## The Seven Realms

**The Realm of Light — White**

The Realm of Light, the seat of Light and Prophecy. It is the source: the one, the pure, the origin of eternity, from which every other color was refracted.

**The Ethereal Realms — Black and Orange**

From White came Black and Orange, the two faces of the First Titan:

- The First Titan's Study — Death and Void
- The First Titan's Laboratory — Chaos and Transformation

**The Mortal Realms — Green, Yellow, Red, and Blue**

From Black and Orange came Green and Yellow:

- **Terria** — Earth and Life
- **Aerolon** — Air and Lightning

From Green and Yellow came Red and Blue:

- **Sanguia** — Fire and Blood
- **Aquia** — Water and Illusion

The descent runs in two lineages from the Light. The **Black line** runs Black → Green → Red: death gives rise to earth and life, and life to fire and blood. The **Orange line** runs Orange → Yellow → Blue: chaos gives rise to air and lightning, and the storm to water. These are the inner weave of the septisigil. Its perimeter ring — White, Yellow, Red, Orange, Black, Blue, Green — is a different thing entirely: the cycle by which the schools combine in practice, described in Book IV. The two drawings share one figure because they describe one Prism from two sides, descent and adjacency.

Each of the four Mortal Realms carries a single name that means three things at once: the realm, the body in the sky, and the Titan who is both. Terria is the Vine; Aerolon is the Bird; Sanguia is the Mountain; Aquia is the Serpent. These are the names mortals use. Every Titan also bears a true name that mortals were never built to hold — **Ascarthasseal** is Aquia's, **Cyndrassaneal** is Sanguia's, and the true names of Terria and Aerolon are not known. The Titans themselves are the subject of Book VI, The Pantheon.

## The Sky

The sky over Home is the Shattering made visible. The sun anchors the system and the moon rides with Home; the seven realms wander between and beyond them. Counted outward from the sun, the bodies are:

1. **Chaos** — innermost; red, orange, and black
2. **Fire** — red
3. **Home** — the habitable world, carrying the moon
4. **Earth** — brown and green
5. **Light** — the white gas giant
6. **Air** — the ringed planet; white and pale blue
7. **Water** — Neptune blue
8. **Death** — outermost; black, occulted behind the star, known mostly by its shadow crossing the galactic cloud

Chaos and Fire transit the sun. Air carries the sky's one ring system. Every one of the seven realms shows the same apparent size from Home, about a quarter of the disc of the sun or the moon, so that no realm outranks another in the eye of the watcher.

Each realm is one school's seat, and each school reads its own planet: Chaos is Orange, Fire is Red, Earth is Green, Light is White, Air is Yellow, Water is Blue, and Death is Black. The moon alone belongs to no school. It is the one real thing in a sky of reflections: it keeps the true synodic cycle, phase for phase and place for place, with the Moon of the world outside the game. Home turns on a day far shorter than a real one; the exact length of that day is the designer's to set, and is still being tuned.

**The sky is law.** Every event in the world is a configuration of the heavens first and a happening on the ground second. Alignments, eclipses, and transits are never scripted and never random; they emerge from real orbits, and those who learn the stars can see what is coming and name the day. The effects are global: an eclipse changes the whole of Home at once, from the deepest dungeon to the quietest farm, and scarcity and abundance are foretold in the sky before they arrive on the ground. The design never runs the other way. Colors, stats, events, people, bodies, magic, Titans — all of it is one structure, all reflections of the same Prism.

## Home

Home is one world, and it is a large one, the size of the real Earth. No player will ever walk all of it. It is a sphere tiled in hexagons, and such a sphere cannot be tiled in hexagons alone: twelve points on its face are pentagons, where the tiling will not close. Those twelve points are not part of the ordinary world. They are the **Advent locations**.

An Advent location is set beyond the survivable line by design: atop a peak no road climbs, or beneath a sea no hull has crossed. The country around each one is wrong in the way that sailors' stories are wrong — compasses that will not settle, weather that answers to no season, landmarks that are not where they were. To enter one is to leave Home. These are the doors the Titans built when they cast the realms into the sky, and the Titans are their wardens still. The Shattering made a prison as surely as it made a sky, and the Advent gates are the guarded gates of that prison; whether they were built to keep something in or to keep mortals out is a question the schools have argued for a hundred thousand years without settling. Reaching one is the hardest thing an adventurer can attempt, and the first breach is the hardest of all. What lies through a gate is not Home. It is a realm, or the road to one: a place made by hand, where the rules of the world shift under your feet.

There is no other way from Home to the realms, and no ordinary reason to want one. Every school teaches on Home. Every trade is carried on Home. The realms are the sources of the light, not places to live, and the only mortals who have ever stood in one went through a door with a Titan in it.

## The Schools of Magic

There are **seven schools** of magic, one for each color, and each school is the institution on Home that teaches the color its realm pours down. Every school holds two **Domains**, the two aspects of its color: Red is Fire and Blood, Green is Earth and Life, and so on through the seven. Within each Domain lie the **disciplines** — the kinds of spell, such as Abjuration, Divination, Pyromancy, and Thaumaturgy, catalogued in Book IV — and every spell belongs to a discipline. A discipline is a skill; a Domain is the road into a school; mastering the school itself is the work of a lifetime, and a long-lived people may manage two.

| Color | School's Domains | Statistic | Philosophy |
|---|---|---|---|
| White | Light and Prophecy | Wisdom | Enlightenment or Revelation |
| Yellow | Air and Lightning | Dexterity | Courage or Audacity |
| Red | Fire and Blood | Strength | Valor or Conquest |
| Orange | Chaos and Transformation | Luck | Diligence or Cunning |
| Blue | Water and Illusion | Intelligence | Knowledge or Truth |
| Green | Earth and Life | Endurance | Perseverance or Hunger |
| Black | Death and Void | Constitution | Transcendence or Ambition |

**The seven statistics.** Each school answers to one statistic, and the seven together are the whole measure of a character. A statistic is a capacity, never an action: Strength is the capacity to strike, Constitution the capacity to be struck, Endurance the capacity to keep going, and so on. Charm is something a character does rather than something a character has, which is why there is no Charisma; where a game would reach for it, Prism reaches for Luck. Constitution and Endurance are never the same stat, because being hit and sustaining effort are different capacities. Every statistic must matter in a fight; combat on Home is the deliberate, punishing, action combat of the Souls lineage, and nothing a character is built from is allowed to be decorative.

**The philosophies.** Every color carries a virtue, and every virtue can be approached from two directions. One pole approaches the virtue collectively and impersonally; the other approaches it through the self — inherited, claimed, made one's own. Blue's Knowledge is the shared, tested, factual account of the world; Blue's Truth is the account a person has made their own and holds against all comers. Neither pole is named as the right one, in the schools or in the design. We do not preach; we reveal. A school's two Domains each contain followers of both poles, and a player's own choices, pole by pole, express in the settlements their line builds: a line that walks the path of the self breeds settlements wary of outsiders, and a line that walks the collective path breeds a different strength and a different weakness. Which is better is the player's conclusion to draw, never the book's.

The four virtues of the mortal schools — Valor, Courage, Knowledge, and Perseverance — are the traits the first mortals grew in their earliest generations, and they are the engine of the mortal realms to this day.

**Life is Green.** Life magic belongs to the Green school, the domain of the god of the Vine: the magic of green and growing things. White is Light and Prophecy only. Healing therefore splits along the seam between them: White wards and protects through Thaumaturgy, and Green grows and mends through Biomancy.

**Black combines with nothing.** Every school finds its allies around the ring of the septisigil, save one. Black is the sole exception, and the reason is in its nature: Death and Void are the absence of, and an absence cannot invent or create on its own. Black reflects, and Black reanimates what already lived elsewhere in the spectrum, but it brings nothing new of its own to any pairing. Book IV describes what this costs a Black mage and what it gives them instead.

## The Principalities

The three tiers of magic are divided not by permission but by kind.

**White is divine.** Light and Prophecy are not made and not taught; they are invoked, the channelling of eternity itself. The magic of White belongs to the **Principality of White** — the source, the one, the pure, the origin of eternity — which is not an embodied Titan and casts no constellation.

**Black and Orange are titanic.** They are the First Titan's own two faces, Death and Chaos, the hidden domains that were never made for anyone. The schools of Black and Orange belong to the **First Titan, the Two-Faced God** of the constellations.

**The four elemental colors were made for mortals.** Earth, Air, Fire, and Water are the only magics that were ever created for someone's benefit, and the only ones that descend through a chain of hands. The elemental Titans refracted them from the Light and taught them to the ancient immortals, the long-lived children of the Titans who walked the early world. Those immortals were the original patrons of the mortal magics, and their names are lost to the centuries. They in turn gifted the magics to the first humans, whose descendants hold the mortal Principalities today — and whose identities are, in their own way, as unrecorded as their teachers'. Each mortal color still answers to its Titan as its source: Green to the Vine, Yellow to Aerolon, Red to Sanguia, Blue to Aquia.

One discipline stands outside the chain. **Necromancy is inherited, not taught.** It does not descend Titan to immortal to human at all; a limited set of peoples carry it in the blood, and the Dark Elves are among them. This gates one discipline, not the Black school or the First Titan's ownership of it, and it is why the Muse's own line commanded the dead long before she ever stood at a school's gate.

## The Peoples of Prism

The peoples of Prism are the ten figures of the Player Cast card (`Prism/Concept/PrismRaces/PrismPlayerCastV2.png`), which is the authority for their build, their skin, and their stature relative to one another. These ten are the playable peoples. Left to right on the card:

| # | People | Stature | Build |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Elf** | the tallest — well over six feet | gaunt, pale, hairless; long pointed ears |
| 2 | **Half-Orc** | a head over a human | massive, olive-grey, tusked |
| 3 | **Human** | the reference build | medium, athletic |
| 4 | **Half-Elf** | a little under a human | slender, pale, pointed ears; between Elf and Human |
| 5 | **Dark-Elf** | a little under a Half-Elf | slim, indigo-skinned, pointed ears |
| 6 | **Lizardman** | about a human, hunched, tailed | scaled olive-bronze; clawed, digitigrade feet |
| 7 | **Dwarf** | about half a human; the tallest of the short peoples | very broad, heavily muscled, full beard |
| 8 | **Halfling** | about half a human, a little under the Dwarf | slight and slim |
| 9 | **Hobbit** | the shortest of the three short peoples | stocky, tending chubby |
| 10 | **Fae** | tiny; knee-high to a human | winged, luminous |

Halflings and Hobbits are distinct peoples; hobbits tend chubby, and halflings do not. The card carries a scale, but the book gives no numbers for height: the peoples are sized against one another, and the card decides. The Dark-Elf's indigo skin is the ruled look — the dark elf of fantasy, not a human tone. The card's labels are the canonical spellings.

Three of the ten are already written into the world's story. The **Dark-Elves** are the Lonely Muse's own people: her line, the D'Vixx, commanded Necromancy by blood, and that inheritance is part of why she arrived at every school's gate already set apart. The **Lizardmen** stand in her story as well, in the person of Jonas, the lizardman monk of her childhood. The **Dwarves** are the earth-dwellers Book IV's bestiary vocabulary always assumed; their existence as a people is settled by the card.

**Longevity is the axis along which the peoples differ.** Race is how a family experiences time. A long-lived people earns a larger pool of experience across one lifetime — enough to master two schools in a single life where a human masters one — and trades generational turnover for continuity; a short-lived people cycles through the family tapestry faster, and feels the dynasty systems of Book I as a rhythm rather than a horizon. The peoples have real and differing strengths and limits, and the world engineers conflict between them without ever ruling on who is right: standing between peoples is a thing a family earns and spends through play, not a verdict the design hands down.

## What Remains Unwritten

The chronicles of Home are old, and they have gaps. Some of those gaps are deliberate, and some are simply not yet filled.

What became of the Lonely Muse is the first and largest. Some say the Titans destroyed her with the unity that made her; some say she sleeps in one of the seven realms, and that the gates were built to hold her there; some say she walks Home still, and that the sky has not finished telling her story. The schools do not agree, and the book does not decide.

The roster of peoples is canon but not closed: a cat people, and perhaps others, are still to be considered. Whether the kobolds and gnomes of the old bestiary are peoples in their own right or only names for things that live underground is not settled. How the Elves, Half-Elves, and Dark-Elves of the present age relate to the long-lived, elf-like peoples of the Muse's era — descendants, the same peoples, or neither — is not recorded, and neither are the lifespans of the ten peoples in years, nor where on Home each of them makes its home, nor the houses, guilds, and nations in which a family tapestry sits.

What lies through an Advent gate is made by hand and described nowhere here, and the exact working of the gates themselves — how one opens, whether it can close, whether an Advent event might seal a realm away from Home for a season — is the sky's secret for now.
