## Introduction

*Assembled by Corvane, archivist, from the field-notes of a hundred hands.*

### What this book is

This is a register of the living, the unliving, and the unreal things of Prism — the beasts that graze and hunt the wilds, the monsters that lair in its dark places, the terrors that crown them, and the bodiless phenomena that haunt the spaces between. For each I have set down what it is, how it behaves, what it can do to you and what it cannot bear done to it, and — where anything is left worth taking — what may be cut, drawn, or salvaged from its remains.

I have tried throughout to say plainly what a thing *does*, and to leave the poetry to the marginalia. A hunter with this book open should know, in the time it takes to read a page, whether to nock an arrow, draw a blade, light a torch, or turn and run.

### Who should read it

Three kinds of reader, and a fourth who should know better.

Adventurers, who mean to survive what they meet, and who will find here the difference between a thing that will flee and a thing that will not. Dungeon Makers, choosing what to raise and where, and how to make a room itself a threat — for the last chapter is as much a builder's kit as a naturalist's catalogue. Herbalists, hunters, and artificers, after the harvest, for whom the Materials of an entry are the whole of its interest. And the merely curious, who are gently warned that curiosity is how the greater part of this book came to be written, and how a good number of its contributors came to stop contributing.

### Where to turn with questions

For the colors and the schools that order this register, and the Principalities that own them, see Book V. For raising, recruiting, and building with these creatures, and for the deep-past constructs that ride in on a builder's packs, see Book II. For the world that grows them and the ecology that decides where each is found, see Book III. For the magic and the named effects the entries invoke — the roots and snares and fears — see Book IV. Corrections, disputes, and new sightings are to be brought to the Archive; bring the carcass if you can, and a witness who is not yourself if you cannot.

## The Seven Colors and the Shadow Between

The world is ordered in seven colors, and every creature in it carries the grain of the one it belongs to. This is not a scheme I laid over the beasts to sort them; it is the sorting the world was already born with. A thing is White or Red or Black the way a stone is heavy — in its nature, before anyone thinks to name it.

- **White — Light and Prophecy.** The austere and the radiant. Guardians that do not tire, seers broken on what they foresaw, and a brightness that reveals rather than warms. Its beasts keep the high, clean places; its terrors keep the law, and are not merciful about it.
- **Yellow — Air and Lightning.** Storm and open sky. The fliers and the things that own the air — harpies, griffins, the great rocs and the thunder itself given wings — and below them the swift, wary fauna of the windswept plains.
- **Red — Fire and Blood.** Savagery and war. The burning and the raiding: orcs and ogres, fire that walks, the brutal and the tusked, and the scavengers of the scorched lands that follow them for what they leave.
- **Orange — Chaos and Transformation.** Aberration and mutation — the wrong-made, the shifting, the things assembled against every rule of shape. *Virel writes in the margin here that we call them aberrations only because they do not keep the shape we agreed on, and asks who agreed, and when. I have let the note stand and drawn no conclusion from it.*
- **Green — Earth and Life.** The wilderness and the deep places. Goblinkind in the dark, the great beasts of the old wood, the things that grow instead of heal, and the fauna of the forest floor and the caverns beneath.
- **Blue — Water and Illusion.** Depth and deception. Drowners, shape-thieves, and luring voices; the deep serpents and the deceits that walk ashore wearing a stranger's face.
- **Black — Death and Void.** The absence. Nothing here is made — only the dead reflected and raised, wearing the shapes they wore in life. It keeps no beasts of its own, for the dead do not graze.
- **Inverted — the shadow between.** Not a color and not a beast. The non-real: the phantasms, the night-spooks, the effects that wear no body. You do not kill these; you endure them, or you leave.

## On the Nature of Beasts

*A unifying principle, and the common observations of those who have gone looking.*

Every entry in this book falls into one of three ranks, and the ranks hold across all seven colors. **Animals** are the ordinary living — the biome's own fauna, wary and mortal, most of which would rather run than fight. **Monsters** are the marked and the dangerous, the things a traveler names when asked what haunts a place. **Terrors** are the apex and the named, the great beasts and the singular horrors that a whole region tells stories about. Two colors keep the rule loosely: Black holds no Animals, since it raises the dead rather than growing the living, and Inverted holds no creatures at all.

A handful of truths run through the whole register, and I have found it worth setting the naturalists' own words to them.

On the wild, from **Bralt the Huntsman**, who has killed more of the things in this book than any three other contributors and can no longer close his left hand: *"Every animal in here will run. Most monsters will not. Learn which is which before you learn their names — the names keep. You may not."*

On the harvest, from **Sister Verda of the Vine**, herbalist: *"There is no beast so foul that no part of it will mend, feed, or kill — the whole art is in knowing which part, and in taking it before the thing has quite finished dying. Waste nothing. The world does not, and the world made these."* She adds, of the venomous and the rotting: *"Decay and poison are not their own kind of creature. They are a shadow that can fall on any flesh. Assume it has."*

On the dead, from **Ossian the Mortist**, keeper of the Black register: *"Understand this before you read a page of my chapter. The dead invent nothing. Whatever wears a horror's shape in the dark of a barrow, something wore it first, in the light, and lived. Grieve for that. Do not grieve for what you are about to put down."*

On the seen and the unseen, from **Corvane**, who compiled this book and trusts none of it entirely: *"I have set down what a hundred hands swore they saw. I believe half of them saw truly. The other half met something from the last chapter, which is not in the habit of showing you what is there."*

And from **Brother Lucen of the Dawn**, on the color that opens the book: *"Do not mistake White for a comfort. The light does not warm; it reveals, and revelation is not kind. What burns in these pages under that heading would sooner blind a pilgrim than bless him."*

Read, then, in that spirit. Name the thing, weigh the rank, and remember Bralt: the names keep.

## White — Light and Prophecy

Austere and radiant rather than warm. Critters are ordinary wildlife of bright, high terrain; monsters and terrors are White-native.

### Animals

#### 1. Mountain Goat
**Environments:** high crags and pale cliffs.

**Description:** A stocky wild goat adapted to sheer rock faces, its coat bleached pale gray-white by constant exposure to sun and snow-glare. Adults stand chest-high with short, backward-curving horns used more for territorial headbutting among themselves than defense against predators. Common wherever the terrain climbs high enough that most other animals give up.

**Behaviors:** Grazes in small family groups of three to six, always keeping at least one animal watching from a higher vantage point. At the first sign of danger the group scatters uphill along routes only they can navigate, using ledges and drops that discourage all but the most determined pursuit. They do not fight except among themselves during rutting season, and rarely to serious injury even then.

**Threats & Defenses:** Poses no meaningful threat to an armed traveler and will not initiate contact. Its real defense is terrain — pursuit across the goat's home ground risks a fall for anything without comparable footing. A cornered goat will headbutt (a minor knockback, Sunder-lite to stance), but only as a last resort.

#### 2. Snow Hare
**Environments:** open snowfields and pale scrub.

**Description:** A small, wide-eyed hare whose coat pales to near-white in bright, cold terrain, patterned faintly enough to break up its outline against snow and pale stone. Almost entirely silent, communicating danger to others of its kind through sudden movement rather than sound.

**Behaviors:** Freezes completely at the first hint of a threat, relying on stillness and camouflage before committing to flight. Once it breaks, it moves in a rapid, unpredictable zigzag that makes clean pursuit nearly impossible for anything not built for short, explosive bursts of speed. Flees at the first opportunity rather than risk any confrontation.

**Threats & Defenses:** No offensive capability whatsoever. Its survival strategy is entirely evasive — extremely difficult to strike while fleeing, and quick to vanish into terrain features too small for a pursuer to follow.

#### 3. Highland Eagle
**Environments:** high cliffs, open sky.

**Description:** A broad-winged raptor with pale gray-white plumage, built for riding the thin, cold air above the highest cliffs. Its nest, built on the most inaccessible ledge it can find, is reused across many years and grows into a considerable structure of bone and branch.

**Behaviors:** Hunts from great height, circling for long stretches before folding into a near-vertical dive on anything smaller that moves in the open below. Solitary outside mating pairs, and largely ignores anything that isn't potential prey — but becomes fiercely aggressive, diving repeatedly, if its nest is approached.

**Threats & Defenses:** A diving strike targets exposed flesh and can inflict a brief Expose from the impact and the talon-rake that follows. Weak and slow once grounded — anything that survives the initial dive has the clear advantage in a prolonged exchange.

#### 4. Pale Wolf
**Environments:** open highlands, pack hunter.

**Description:** A lean wolf variant with a light, almost silvery coat, common across open, exposed highland country where darker fur offers no advantage. Packs range widely and mark territory aggressively against rivals, though they avoid settled areas when they can.

**Behaviors:** Hunts in coordinated packs of three to six, using terrain and numbers to separate a single target before committing. A pack tests its target from a distance — feints, circling, brief lunges — before one member commits to a genuine strike, using the resulting opening for the rest to close in.

**Threats & Defenses:** Individually unremarkable, but a full pack can encircle and effectively Snare a target through sheer positioning, cutting off retreat before the real attack begins. Packs break off quickly once the numbers turn against them — they do not fight to the last wolf.

#### 5. White Owl
**Environments:** open ground, ruins, high rafters.

**Description:** A large owl with pale, mottled plumage that renders it nearly invisible against snow, pale stone, or moonlit cloud. Its flight is silent even at close range, a product of soft-edged feathers that break up the turbulence most birds produce.

**Behaviors:** Hunts alone at dusk and dawn from a fixed perch, watching a hunting ground for long stretches before a single decisive strike. Avoids any confrontation with something larger than its usual prey and abandons a hunt entirely rather than risk injury.

**Threats & Defenses:** No meaningful offense against anything human-sized or larger. Its real relevance to a party is as an early warning — its sudden flight from a perch is often the first sign something else has entered the area.

#### 6. Snow Leopard
**Environments:** rocky high slopes.

**Description:** A large, solitary cat with thick pale fur patterned in faint rosettes, built for climbing and ambush rather than open pursuit. Ranges across a wide territory of rocky high ground, marking it with scent and claw far more often than it's actually seen.

**Behaviors:** Stalks prey at length from cover, closing the distance slowly before a single committed pounce. If that first strike fails to bring the target down, it withdraws rather than press a fight it's no longer certain to win — it does not fight for pride.

**Threats & Defenses:** The pounce itself is the danger — a solid hit inflicts an Expose effect as claws rake for purchase before it disengages. Poor stamina for a prolonged fight; anything that survives the opening attack and keeps up the pressure will usually see it break off.

#### 7. Mountain Ram
**Environments:** steep crags.

**Description:** A heavy-set wild ram with thick, curling horns built for repeated high-force impacts, found wherever there's enough exposed rock for its kind to establish dominance contests. Its hide is scarred from a lifetime of headlong collisions with rivals.

**Behaviors:** Grazes placidly in loose herds most of the year, but turns aggressive and territorial during mating season, charging any intruder — rival, predator, or traveler — that comes too close to its claimed ground. Outside that season it avoids conflict entirely.

**Threats & Defenses:** A charge is a genuine hazard on narrow terrain — the impact functions as a stance-breaking knockback, and a target driven off a ledge or into a fall takes the real damage from the charge, not the ram itself. It has no other combat repertoire and disengages the moment its charge fails to connect.

### Monsters

#### 8. Pegasus
**Environments:** high peaks, clean air.

**Description:** A winged horse of considerable size, its feathers pale and coat near-white, found only where the air runs clean and the ground beneath it has never been worked by human hands. Intelligent well beyond an ordinary horse, and unmistakably aware of who approaches and why.

**Behaviors:** Claims a high, untouched territory and defends it, but does not hunt and largely ignores travelers who pass through without lingering or disturbing the ground. It occasionally allows itself to be approached, and rarer still, ridden — but a rider who proves cruel or careless is thrown, hard, and not given a second chance.

**Threats & Defenses:** Its primary weapon is a powerful double-kick capable of Sundering light armor outright. It fights only to drive off an intruder, not to kill, and disengages by simply taking to the air once the threat is removed — pursuit on foot is pointless. Resists Fear; nothing on the ground unsettles a creature that can leave the ground behind at will.

#### 9. Unicorn
**Environments:** deep untouched forest.

**Description:** A horned horse, solitary by nature and ancient in bearing regardless of its actual age, found only in the oldest, least-disturbed stretches of forest. Its horn is said to react to poison and corruption before either can take hold — whether this is literally true or simply the reputation that follows the creature is argued endlessly by those who study it.

**Behaviors:** Avoids all contact where it can, retreating deeper into cover at the first sign of an approach rather than confronting it. It will not be lured, bribed, or coerced into companionship, and treats any attempt to capture or corner it as the gravest possible provocation.

**Threats & Defenses:** A cornered unicorn's horn-strike Sunders magical wards and enchantments outright, treating protective magic as something to be pierced rather than fought around. It is near-immune to Poison and Disease, a resistance that extends briefly to anything fighting beside it, though inconsistently enough that it shouldn't be relied on.

#### 10. Sphinx
**Environments:** guards a fixed place or object.

**Description:** A great maned beast with a broad, powerfully built body and an unnervingly intelligent, almost human gaze. It selects a single place, object, or piece of knowledge to guard and remains there for what can be centuries, apparently untroubled by time, weather, or hunger.

**Behaviors:** Confronts any intruder verbally before it considers violence, posing a riddle or question relevant to whatever it guards. It waits, seemingly indefinitely, for an answer, showing no impatience or malice during the exchange — the moment for violence only arrives after a wrong answer, an attempted deception, or an outright refusal to engage.

**Threats & Defenses:** A failed answer or attempted trickery Silences the offender, their own words in a sense turned against them and rendered unspeakable for a time. In combat it fights with claw and weight, methodical rather than frenzied, and resists both Confound and Charm — nothing about its judgment can be talked or tricked around once made.

#### 11. Couatl
**Environments:** old libraries, places of learning, high ruins.

**Description:** A long, winged serpent with iridescent pale plumage running the length of its body, moving through the air with a slow, deliberate grace that belies real speed when it chooses to use it. Most often found coiled among old shelves, collapsed archives, or the ruins of places once dedicated to learning.

**Behaviors:** Largely ignores intruders who handle what it guards with care, even allowing careful study or copying of texts it watches over. It reacts only to damage, theft, or destruction, at which point it moves to intercept with startling speed for something that, moments before, appeared entirely at rest.

**Threats & Defenses:** Constricts a target in its coils (a Root effect) while it works to disarm or subdue rather than kill outright, consistent with a creature that would rather recover what was taken than punish the thief. Resists Charm and Confound — whatever guards its judgment isn't easily talked around, tricked, or dazzled.

#### 12. Will-o-Wisp
**Environments:** wherever travelers lose the path at first light.

**Description:** A small, drifting mote of pale light, seen most often at first light in places where travelers commonly lose their way. No two accounts of its origin agree, and it gives no sign of possessing anything like a fixed intention.

**Behaviors:** Drifts ahead of anyone who notices it, seeming to invite following. Where it actually leads varies unpredictably — sometimes to a safe path around a hazard, sometimes directly into one — and there is no reliable way to tell which in advance.

**Threats & Defenses:** Effectively untouchable in direct confrontation, blinking short distances the instant it's threatened. A close approach can trigger a sudden flare bright enough to inflict a minor Confound, though whether this is a defense or simply what the wisp does is unclear.

#### 13. Radiant Guardian *(Construct)*
**Environments:** warded thresholds, sealed vaults.

**Description:** An animated suit of pale, light-forged armor or a carved statue given motion — accounts differ on which is more common — faceless and silent except for the ring of metal or stone in motion. It stands or patrols a fixed warded space indefinitely, showing no sign of wear, boredom, or fatigue no matter how long it's been stationed there.

**Behaviors:** Remains motionless or on a slow, repeating patrol until its warded space is actually breached, at which point it moves to engage without hesitation, warning, or any attempt at communication. It does not pursue beyond the boundary of what it was set to guard, breaking off cleanly the instant an intruder retreats past that line.

**Threats & Defenses:** Fights with mechanical, repetitive precision rather than adaptability, favoring heavy, direct strikes. Having no mind of its own, it is entirely immune to Fear, Panic, and Charm. Its outer shell is its real defense — until cracked by a solid Sunder, the light-core that actually animates it is fully protected.

#### 14. Cursed Oracle
**Environments:** sites of an old, unresolved prophecy.

**Description:** A prophet blinded — whether by their own power or by whatever they foresaw is never fully clear — haunting the site of a foretelling that was never resolved. Their eyes are pale and sightless, but they react to an approaching presence long before any normal sense would allow it.

**Behaviors:** Attacks on sight rather than offering any warning, speaking in broken, half-finished fragments of prophecy throughout the fight, addressing things that aren't present or events that haven't happened yet. There is no reasoning with it and no way to predict from its words what it will do next.

**Threats & Defenses:** Its fragmented utterances carry a genuine Confound effect at range, the listener's own sense of the moment briefly scrambled along with the oracle's. Resists Silence — whatever compels its speech isn't something that can simply be shut off.

#### 15. Hollow Zealot
**Environments:** ruins of failed shrines.

## Orange — Chaos and Transformation

The First Titan's Laboratory: aberration, mutation, and things assembled wrong on purpose. ## Yellow — Air and Lightning

Storm and open sky. The animals are ordinary fauna of windswept storm-steppe — high open plains, big skies, grass bent flat under the wind — while the monsters and terrors are the things that own the air above it: winged predators, storm-born elementals, and the great fliers that treat the ground as nothing but a place their prey can't escape to.

#### 1. Falcon
**Environments:** open sky over plains and mesas.

**Description:** A compact, sharp-winged raptor built for speed rather than soaring, its plumage barred in browns and grays that vanish against a cloudy sky. Where an eagle circles and waits, a falcon climbs and stoops, killing with the shock of the dive itself.

**Behaviors:** Hunts alone or in a mated pair from great height, folding into a near-vertical dive the instant it commits to a target below. Largely indifferent to anything too big to take, but fiercely defensive of a nest, harrying intruders with repeated stoops until they leave.

**Threats & Defenses:** A stooping strike hits with real force for its size and can inflict a brief Expose, but it poses no danger to anything human-sized. Fast and hard to catch in the air; fragile and near-helpless once grounded.

## Green — Earth and Life

Wilderness, endurance, and the deep places. The animals are ordinary fauna of old-growth forest — deep woods, root-tangled hollows, and the caverns beneath — while the monsters and terrors are what the wild breeds where it's oldest and least disturbed: goblinkind in the dark, things that grow instead of heal, and the great beasts of the deep wood.

#### 1. Red Deer
**Environments:** forest, woodland edge, clearings.

**Description:** A graceful woodland deer, the stags crowned with branching antlers, russet-coated and long-legged. It is the common large grazer of the deep woods, wary and quick.

**Behaviors:** Grazes in herds at the forest edge and in clearings, always alert, melting into cover at the first alarm. Stags spar over territory and mates in the rut, and a cornered one will strike with antler and hoof, but flight is always the first choice.

**Threats & Defenses:** A cornered stag's antler-thrust and hoof-strike can injure, but it poses no threat unless trapped. Its defense is speed, alertness, and knowing the wood — it's gone before most predators finish deciding to chase.

## Black — Death and Void

The absence of. Nothing in this set is created — every entry is a reflection or reanimation of something that once lived, per the Black/Void constraint. There are no animals here: the dead do not return as wildlife but as raised, rotting, or hollow echoes of what they were, and the intelligences that raise them. Undead surface wherever the dead were left, in any terrain, so this set is biome-agnostic.

Two conventions run through the whole set. **Life-drain** (wight, wraith, vampire) is a damage property, not a status effect — the attack wounds the target and heals the undead in the same stroke. **Paralysis** (ghoul) maps to Stun and Root. And most undead carry natural immunities: all are immune to Poison and Disease (already dead), the mindless ones (skeleton, zombie, and their kin) are immune to Fear and Charm, and the incorporeal ones (specter, wraith) shrug off ordinary physical harm.

**Description:** A reanimated human skeleton, dry bone held together by the necromancy that raised it, moving with a horrible economy and no flesh to slow it. Empty sockets and a fixed grin are all the face it has, and it neither tires nor flinches.

**Behaviors:** Follows the simple, relentless imperative it was raised with — guard this place, kill that intruder — with no fear, no hesitation, and no self-preservation whatsoever. It fights until destroyed and does not retreat, break, or negotiate; a raised host of them is dangerous through sheer tireless numbers.

**Threats & Defenses:** Fights with whatever blade, spear, or bow it was armed with. Having no flesh, it resists Expose — there is nothing to bleed or tear — and being mindless, it is immune to Fear, Charm, Poison, and Disease. Its dry bones are brittle, though: heavy, crushing blows shatter it far more easily than they would a living body.

## Inverted — The Void Phenomena

Not a bestiary. Inverted is the psionics set — the void group, the non-real, the phantasms and night spooks — and nothing in it has a body to fight. Each entry is a **localized phenomenon**: an effect-zone with a cause but no creature, all signal and no source-you-can-see. You do not kill these; you endure them, leave their zone, or find and disrupt whatever generates them.

**These entries use the Phenomenon template, not the creature template:**

- **Name**
- **Class** — Kinetic (mind moving matter and energy) or Pathic (mind reaching into mind), and the domain within (telekinetic, pyrokinetic, telepathic, etc.).
- **Manifestation** — what the player *perceives*: particles, sound, screen and audio corruption. This is where "no body" gets expressed — the phenomenon is only ever its signal.
- **Zone & Trigger** — where it activates and how (proximity, a bounded space, a provocation), and how it builds.
- **Effect** — the mechanical result, drawn from the same status taxonomy as everything else (Root, Snare, Slow, Expose, Poison/Disease, Stun, Silence, Confound, Sunder, Fear, Panic, Charm), plus the diegetic screen/audio corruption that is this set's signature.
- **Source & Counter** — deliberately variable (no fixed rule). A given phenomenon may have *no findable source* (a thin place in the world), a *bound source* (a cursed object or scarred place — destroy the anchor), or *a body* perceivable with the right magic and destroyable with the right tools. The right divination is what tells them apart.
- **Encounter Use** — how a Dungeon Maker bolts the phenomenon onto a boss fight or room. This set is also the psychic-effect construction kit: a room can be built to *embody* an effect, and a boss encounter can trigger one as a phase.

There are no Weak/Normal/Strong/Boss tiers here. Each phenomenon scales by **Intensity** (effect strength, zone size, and how reachable the source is), given as a low → high band.

Inverted has no bosses of its own — a phenomenon is a hazard, not a foe. But any terror in the color sets can *use* these effects, which is what makes this set double as the boss-fight kit.

### Kinetics — mind moving matter and energy

#### 1. Poltergeist
**Class:** Kinetic — Telekinetic.

**Manifestation:** No figure, nothing to point a weapon at — only the room turning hostile. It begins small: a cup slides off a shelf, a door rattles in its frame, a chill and a felt *pressure* as if the air has thickened. Then it escalates — loose objects lift and hang trembling, furniture drags itself across the floor with a shriek of wood, and everything not bolted down begins to fly. The only visual tell of the force itself is a faint refractive shimmer around each object as it's seized, there and gone. The sound is the dread: scraping, rattling, the sudden gunshot bang of something hurled into the wall beside your head.

**Zone & Trigger:** Bound to a specific space — a room, a hall, a cellar. It wakes on intrusion or on a particular provocation (disturbing a grave-good, speaking a name, the fall of night), and it builds: the longer the living remain, the more it lifts and the harder it throws. Leaving the bounded space ends the barrage at once; it does not follow.

**Effect:** Hurled objects strike for impact damage with a chance to Stun on a heavy hit; a sustained barrage can Snare a target where they stand, pinned and battered, and larger flung furniture applies knockback. At low intensity it is harassment and atmosphere; at its peak, a genuine kill-zone of flying mass.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a thin place with no source (only endured and escaped); a cursed object or charged relic anchoring it (destroy the anchor and it collapses); or a latent, grieving, or malevolent psion nearby, unseen, driving it (perceivable with the right magic, killable with the right tools).

**Encounter Use:** Overlay on a boss arena so the room itself joins the fight — debris on a timer, or triggered when the boss is wounded. Standalone, it works as a haunt, a hazard-puzzle (find the anchor), or a pressure mechanic that punishes lingering.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a few rattling, sliding objects, barely harmful. *Mid* — a steady hail of thrown debris, a room you want out of. *High* — a storm of hurled furniture and stone, lethal to stand in, worsening the longer you stay.

#### 2. Cold Hollow
**Class:** Kinetic — Cryokinetic.

**Manifestation:** The temperature falls without cause. Breath fogs, then frost creeps across stone and steel, feathering outward from a center that isn't anything — no ice-thing, no source, just cold spreading like a stain. The air aches to breathe. At its heart, surfaces go white and brittle, and the last of the warmth drains out of everything, including you.

**Zone & Trigger:** A slowly spreading region, growing outward from a fixed origin point over time. It doesn't lunge or trigger — it simply expands, and the danger is failing to notice how far it has crept while you lingered.

**Effect:** A killing cold that inflicts frost damage-over-time to anything within the zone and Slows those it grips as limbs stiffen. Deeper toward the center, the cold bites harder and the Slow deepens toward a Root.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — an unquiet place that simply bleeds cold; a frost-charged relic at the origin (destroy it to halt the spread); or a dying cryokinetic mind somewhere near the center. Fire and warmth push the edge back; leaving outpaces it, since it spreads slowly.

**Encounter Use:** A creeping-hazard room that forces movement and denies a safe corner, or a boss-arena layer that shrinks the usable floor as the fight drags on.

**Intensity:** *Low* — an uncomfortable chill, minor drain. *Mid* — a spreading frost-zone with real damage and a biting Slow. *High* — a fast, wide killing-cold that Roots and kills those caught in its heart.

#### 3. Spontaneous Flame
**Class:** Kinetic — Pyrokinetic.

**Manifestation:** Heat with no fire to explain it, then fire with no spark. Cloth smolders, hair singes, the air shimmers — and then things simply catch: a curtain, a book, the oil on a blade, and sometimes flesh, igniting as though a flame that was always there has only just become visible. There is no fire-elemental, no burning shape; the flame arrives already halfway to consuming its fuel.

**Zone & Trigger:** Keys on flammable material and on the living within a bounded area, igniting things one after another rather than all at once. Often triggered by a provocation or simply by presence, and it favors whatever burns easiest first.

**Effect:** Ignites gear and surroundings and sets targets burning — a burn damage-over-time — and a spreading fire becomes an environmental hazard in its own right, blocking paths and filling a space with smoke (a lingering Confound from the choking haze).

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a scarred, fire-prone thin place; a pyrokinetic relic; or a hidden mind with a gift for setting the world alight. Water, smothering, and denying it fuel counter it; the source, where there is one, stops the ignitions when destroyed.

**Encounter Use:** A room that punishes flammable gear and slow movement, or a boss phase that sets the arena alight, forcing repositioning through spreading fire.

**Intensity:** *Low* — smoldering and small ignitions, more hazard than harm. *Mid* — objects and targets catching fire, a spreading burn. *High* — a room igniting around you, walls of spontaneous flame and choking smoke.

#### 4. Crushing Weight
**Class:** Kinetic — Geo/Telekinetic.

**Manifestation:** Nothing changes to the eye, but everything grows *heavy*. A pressure settles onto shoulders and chest, the floor seems to pull harder, each step is a labor. Dust presses flat, water bulges downward, and at the center of it a person can be driven to their knees and then to the ground by a force that has no shape and cannot be pushed off.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded field of oppressive force, sometimes fixed, sometimes pulsing in waves. It intensifies toward a center, and the trigger is usually crossing into range or provoking whatever holds the space.

**Effect:** The weight Roots a caught target in place as movement becomes impossible, and at high intensity crushes for damage and can Stun as the pressure spikes. It also Sunders — armor buckles and gear groans under a force nothing can brace against.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a gravity-warped thin place; an anchor-object generating the field; or a geokinetic mind bearing down. Reaching and destroying a source releases the field; short of that, escaping the zone's edge is the only relief, and the weight fights every step toward it.

**Encounter Use:** A room that slows and pins, forcing a fight or a puzzle under pressure; or a boss mechanic that periodically Roots the party in place, setting up the boss's own attacks.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a heavy, sluggish drag, movement taxed. *Mid* — a pinning field that Roots and strains gear. *High* — a crushing core that drives targets down, damages, and Stuns.

#### 5. Howling Pressure
**Class:** Kinetic — Aerokinetic.

**Manifestation:** A wind that isn't weather. It rises in a bounded space with no opening to admit it, howling through a sealed room, tearing at clothing and footing, flinging grit and loose debris in stinging sheets. There is no air-elemental churning at its heart — only the pressure itself, shoving and screaming, with a sound like a gale forced through a keyhole.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded region of violent moving air, often gusting in bursts rather than blowing steadily. It keys on presence and builds, and the gusts can come from shifting directions, denying any stable stance.

**Effect:** Battering gusts apply knockback, shoving targets off balance and toward hazards, and the constant pressure Slows movement against it. Airborne grit and debris can Expose on the worst gusts. Its danger is as much in *where* it throws you — toward a ledge, a pit, another effect — as in the force itself.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a wind-scarred thin place; an aerokinetic relic; or a mind driving the air. Heavy anchoring, cover, and reaching the source counter it; in the open zone, it simply shoves until you're out of it.

**Encounter Use:** A room that pushes toward environmental danger (pits, spikes, another phenomenon), or a boss-arena layer that periodically shoves the party out of position.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a stiff, staggering wind. *Mid* — hard gusts with real knockback and a fighting Slow. *High* — a shrieking maelstrom of pressure that hurls targets bodily across the space.

#### 6. The Maelstrom
**Class:** Kinetic — Telekinetic (the high-intensity ceiling).

**Manifestation:** The full, catastrophic expression of the kinetic phenomena — a zone where the world itself comes apart and takes flight. Everything not anchored lifts: stone, furniture, weapons, water, dust, all of it orbiting and colliding in a roaring, tearing storm around a still and sourceless center. It is what a poltergeist becomes when nothing restrains it, and standing in it is standing inside a thing that is disassembling the room.

**Zone & Trigger:** A large, bounded storm-zone, usually the climax of a haunted place rather than a common hazard. It builds toward a peak and holds, and the trigger is typically a grave provocation — breaking a seal, reaching a forbidden center, killing the wrong thing.

**Effect:** A convergence of kinetic effects at once: constant impact damage from flying mass, Stun from heavy strikes, knockback and Snare from the churning debris, and Sunder as the storm tears at armor and gear. It is less an attack than a place that cannot be survived for long.

**Source & Counter:** Variable, but at this scale usually tied to something momentous — a powerful mind, a great anchor, a deep wound in the world. It is meant to be escaped or shut off at a source, not weathered; there is rarely a way to simply outlast it.

**Encounter Use:** A climactic room or a final boss-phase where the arena itself becomes the threat — survive, reach the anchor, or end the encounter before the storm ends you.

**Intensity:** *High only* — this phenomenon has no low band; it is the ceiling. Its variation is scale: a single room torn apart, or a whole structure coming down.

### Pathy — mind reaching into mind

#### 7. The Whispering
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic.

**Manifestation:** Voices where there are none — at first at the edge of hearing, then closer, then *inside*, murmuring in your own voice and the voices of the people you trust. They tell you things: that the floor ahead is safe when it isn't, that an ally has betrayed you, that something is behind you, that you heard a door you didn't. The audio corrupts subtly — footsteps that aren't yours, a name whispered, a conversation half-caught through a wall that has no room behind it.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded region, keyed on presence; the whispering rises the longer you stay and grows more specific, more personal, and more plausible the more it learns from being near you.

**Effect:** A pervasive Confound — false information injected directly, so that what the player perceives can no longer be fully trusted. It doesn't damage; it *misleads*, and in a dungeon full of real hazards, being told the wrong one is safe is its own kind of lethal.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a thin place that murmurs; a cursed object that whispers; or a telepath, hidden, reaching into minds. Wards against the mind, leaving the zone, or silencing the source counter it; without those, the only defense is distrusting your own senses.

**Encounter Use:** A room that lies to the party — false safe-paths, phantom sounds, seeded paranoia — or a boss mechanic that feeds the party misinformation mid-fight.

**Intensity:** *Low* — occasional false sounds, unsettling. *Mid* — persistent lying voices, real risk of acting on them. *High* — a constant, personal, convincing torrent that makes every perception suspect.

#### 8. Dread
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic (affective).

**Manifestation:** No sound, no sight, nothing at all — which is the horror of it. You cross an invisible line and, for no reason your senses can name, you are *afraid*. The fear mounts with every step deeper, a certainty of doom pressing on the mind with no object and no cause, until standing still is unbearable and going forward is worse.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded field of imposed terror, radiating from a center. It keys purely on presence and intensifies toward the middle; there is nothing to see coming, only the feeling arriving.

**Effect:** Escalating Fear that deepens toward Panic near the center — the mind's own dread turned into a mechanical loss of nerve and, at the worst, of control. It inflicts nothing physical; it simply makes the space psychologically impassable without steadying the mind against it.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a place soaked in old terror; a fear-charged relic; or a mind projecting dread. Wards against the mind and sheer resolve carry a party through; reaching the source, where there is one, lifts it entirely.

**Encounter Use:** A room that must be crossed against mounting Panic, gating progress on nerve rather than combat; or a boss aura that wears down the party's control the longer the fight runs near it.

**Intensity:** *Low* — unease, a reluctance to proceed. *Mid* — real Fear, hard to push through. *High* — Panic near the center, control lost to terror with no visible cause.

#### 9. The Screech
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic / Auditory. *(incorporates the Static Choir)*

**Manifestation:** You hear it before there is anything to see — a thin static rising at the edge of hearing, climbing toward a chorus of screaming that comes from no throat. As it climbs, perception corrupts: the view smears and artifacts, edges tear, colors run wrong, the world stutters like a signal breaking up. There is nothing there. By the time the visual noise peaks with the sound, the screeching is inside your skull, and the body has already started to run.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded region; you cross into range and it builds over several seconds whether or not you understand it. It keys on presence, not sight — walls and darkness are no defense, because it was never a sound in the room. It peaks, holds, and subsides in waves.

**Effect:** Escalates on a curve. The rising static Confounds (screen-corruption, inputs feel wrong); Fear takes hold as the chorus mounts; and at the peak it seizes control outright — forced Panic, the character bolting at random, input withheld until the screech subsides. A brief Stun can punctuate the very peak. This is the most Inverted effect in the set: the "run around out of control while it screams" horror.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a broken psion "singing" without a mouth (a body to find and silence); a cursed relic; or a wound in the world that only screams. Cutting your hearing, leaving the zone, or a Silence strong enough to reach the mind rather than the ear all blunt or end it.

**Encounter Use:** A devastating boss module — trigger a Screech phase so the party scatters, uncontrolled, while the boss presses. As a haunt, it makes a corridor a thing to cross fast and endure rather than explore.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a disorienting hum, a flicker of static; mild Confound. *Mid* — mounting Fear and heavy screen-corruption, hard to act. *High* — the full chorus: the screen tearing apart, Panic seizing control, no input until it ends.

#### 10. The Puppeting
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic (compulsion).

**Manifestation:** The worst kind of quiet. There is no warning and little sensation — a moment's cold thread through the mind, and then your hands are doing something you did not choose. A step toward the edge. A blade turned the wrong way. A door opened that should have stayed shut. The horror is watching your own body obey a will that is not yours, briefly, before it lets go and leaves you to face what it made you do.

**Zone & Trigger:** Keys on a specific target within range, seizing them for a short window rather than blanketing an area. It may strike on entry, on a trigger, or at intervals, and it favors the moment when obedience is most dangerous.

**Effect:** A compulsion — mechanically a Charm with forced movement, taking control of the target for a brief interval and steering them (toward a hazard, away from safety, against an ally). Brief but precisely timed; its danger is entirely in *when* and *where* it seizes you.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — rarely a thin place (compulsion tends to want a will behind it); more often a domination-charged relic, or a controlling mind, hidden, reaching in. Wards against the mind resist it; destroying the source, where there is one, frees everyone at once.

**Encounter Use:** A room that turns the party against itself or steers them into its hazards, or a boss mechanic that briefly seizes a party member to break formation or trigger a trap.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a twitch, a wasted action, quickly shaken. *Mid* — a real seizure of control for a dangerous second or two. *High* — longer, repeated compulsions, party members turned reliably against their own interests.

#### 11. Loom-things
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic (visual phantasm).

**Manifestation:** Shapes at the edge of sight that are wrong when looked at directly. In peripheral vision, something stands in the corner, tall and articulated and *there* — but turn to face it and it resolves into nothing, a coat on a hook, a trick of shadow, a fold in the dark. Look away and it is back, closer. The geometry never quite holds; the thing only exists in the glimpse, and it is always nearer than it was.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded haunted space; the phantasms accumulate the longer you remain and grow bolder, appearing at closer range and in more of the visual field the more the mind strains to catch them.

**Effect:** A Confound built on the classic don't-look-at-it horror — perception corrupts around the shapes, and the strain of tracking things that vanish when faced erodes composure into Fear. It does no direct harm; it degrades the ability to trust what's seen and frays the nerve.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — most often a thin place where the non-real leaks through, sometimes a phantasm-charged relic, rarely a mind. Refusing to engage the shapes, leaving the zone, or wards against illusion counter it; a source, where one exists, ends them when destroyed.

**Encounter Use:** A dread-building room that degrades perception and nerve before a real threat, or a boss-arena layer that fills the edges with false threats to mask the real one.

**Intensity:** *Low* — fleeting corner-of-the-eye shapes, unnerving. *Mid* — persistent, closing phantasms, real Confound and creeping Fear. *High* — a space crowded with wrong geometry, perception barely trustworthy, nerve failing.

#### 12. The Unseen Tally
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic (absence).

**Manifestation:** Detectable only by what's missing. A set of footsteps in the dark that has one too many. A shadow with no owner. A count that comes out wrong — six torches lit, five throwing light. Breath heard when everyone is holding theirs. There is never anything to *see*; there is only the growing, skin-crawling certainty that something is present, measured by the gaps it leaves in a world that should add up and doesn't.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded region; the wrongness accumulates with time and attention, the discrepancies mounting the longer a party stays and the more carefully they look.

**Effect:** Proximity dread that inflicts Fear and, as the sense of an unseen presence sharpens, pushes toward the urge to flee (Panic at its height). No damage, no visible threat — only the unbearable certainty of being counted by something you cannot find.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — characteristically a sourceless thin place (the phenomenon *is* the absence), occasionally an anchor, very rarely a body. Leaving the zone is the reliable answer; wards against the mind steady the nerve; there is often nothing to destroy.

**Encounter Use:** A slow-burn dread room that primes the party before a reveal, or an ambient boss-arena layer that keeps the party unsettled and expecting an attack that comes from the effect's cover.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a single wrong detail, easily dismissed. *Mid* — mounting discrepancies, real and spreading unease. *High* — an overwhelming certainty of a counting presence, nerve breaking toward flight.

#### 13. The Drowning Air
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic (somatic hallucination).

**Manifestation:** You are on dry ground, and you are drowning. The chest seizes, the throat closes, the vision darkens at the edges as though water has closed over your head — but the air is clear and there is nothing in your lungs but the certainty, injected directly into the body, that you cannot breathe. The mind is convinced, and the body obeys the conviction.

**Zone & Trigger:** Keys on targets within a bounded area, imposing the sensation individually. It may build steadily on everyone present or strike a single target at a time, and it favors moments when a held breath is most costly.

**Effect:** A somatic hallucination of suffocation — mechanically a Silence (the victim cannot draw breath to speak or cast) paired with a mounting choke that saps action as the body believes it is drowning. Purely mental in origin, physical in consequence; there is no water to escape, only the belief to break.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a drowned or suffocated thin place; a relic steeped in that death; or a cruel telepathic mind. Wards against the mind and sheer will resist it; leaving the zone or ending the source lifts it — but there is nothing physical to fight, which is much of its horror.

**Encounter Use:** A room that Silences casters and saps the party's wind, or a boss phase that periodically "drowns" the party to deny spellcasting and steady action.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a tightness in the chest, a caught breath. *Mid* — real suffocation-sense, a fighting Silence and drain. *High* — full imposed drowning, casters silenced, the body failing on a lie.

#### 14. The Stutter
**Class:** Pathic — Chronokinetic (the local time-trick).

**Manifestation:** Time comes loose. A few seconds you just lived happen again, slightly wrong — a sound arrives before its cause, a step you took un-takes itself, a companion says a thing you're certain they already said. The world skips and repeats like a scratched groove, and you can no longer be sure whether a moment is the first time or the third, or whether the danger ahead is the danger you already passed.

**Zone & Trigger:** A bounded region where local time frays. It keys on presence and worsens with proximity to a center, the loops growing longer and the disorientation deeper the further in you go. This is the "time tricks in local space" ceiling of the psionic register — small in reach, but genuinely wrong in what it does.

**Effect:** A deep Confound built on temporal disorientation — cause and effect stop lining up, actions seem to repeat or fail to register, and the player loses the thread of what has and hasn't happened. It doesn't damage; it makes the space impossible to navigate or fight in with any certainty.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a time-scarred thin place (the rarest and strangest kind of wound); a chronokinetic relic; or, at the extreme, a mind capable of the psionic ceiling. Wards against the mind help; leaving the zone restores the flow; a source, where one exists, is often buried at the loop's center.

**Encounter Use:** A disorienting puzzle-room where sequence itself is unreliable, or a rare, high-end boss phase that scrambles the party's sense of the fight's timing.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a brief déjà vu, a single skip. *Mid* — repeating seconds, real loss of temporal footing. *High* — sustained looping, cause and effect thoroughly unmoored.

#### 15. The Beckoning
**Class:** Pathic — Telepathic (lure).

**Manifestation:** A pull that feels like your own idea. A conviction arrives, quiet and reasonable, that you *want* to go that way — toward the dark door, down the flooded stair, out onto the thin ice — and the wanting is seamless enough that it doesn't feel imposed at all. Sometimes it dresses itself in a sound just ahead, a light, a familiar voice calling once and not again, always a little further on.

**Zone & Trigger:** Radiates from a point of danger it wants to draw the living toward, keyed on presence within range. The lure strengthens the more it's resisted-without-being-recognized, and it targets the individual, so a party can be split by it.

**Effect:** A Charm-based compulsion toward danger — a psychic will-o-wisp for the mind, steering the victim toward a hazard, a trap, or simply away from the group and into the dark. Subtle where the Puppeting is brute seizure; the Beckoning makes you *choose* to walk into the danger, which is why it so often works.

**Source & Counter:** Variable — a hungry thin place at the end of the lure; a relic that calls; or a mind fishing for the isolated. Recognizing the pull as foreign is the first defense, wards against the mind the second, and destroying a source, where there is one, silences the call.

**Encounter Use:** A room that splits and scatters a party toward its hazards, or a boss mechanic that lures a party member out of position and into a trap or the boss's reach.

**Intensity:** *Low* — a mild, ignorable pull. *Mid* — a persuasive draw, hard to recognize as foreign. *High* — a near-irresistible compulsion toward danger, splitting the party against its own judgment.

# Appendices

*The design and structural detail that orders this book. The register above reads as an in-world catalogue; the appendices below are the editor's and builder's scaffolding — the colors' ownership, the rules every roster obeys, the entry format, and the systems that decide where each creature is found.*

## Appendix A — The Colors and Their Principalities

Each color owns two Domains (Book V), and each belongs to a Principality:

| Color | Domains | Principality |
|---|---|---|
| White | Light, Prophecy | Principality of White |
| Yellow | Air, Lightning | Human |
| Red | Fire, Blood | Human |
| Orange | Chaos, Transformation | First Titan |
| Blue | Water, Illusion | Human |
| Green | Earth, Life | Human |
| Black | Death, Void | First Titan |
| *(Inverted)* | — | Psionics |

The four human Principalities are the schools of the mortal-color domains themselves; their descent (Titan → ancient immortals → human holders) is Book VI canon. The Black and Orange schools both belong to the First Titan, the Two-Faced God — and an emerging idea holds that the Titan's dominant face is its Death/Void (Black) side, with a hidden Chaos (Orange) face behind it that teaches a chosen few a forbidden "true nature." That framing is not yet ratified; see Appendix G.

## Appendix B — The Black/Void Constraint

Black is definitionally *the absence of*. It cannot invent or create, only reflect or reanimate what already lived elsewhere in the spectrum, which is why Black combines with no other school. A lich is "a skeleton of, reanimated from, but not living" — it remembers being alive only in fragments. Ghosts and wraiths are, in the same register, holes shaped like people. This governs every Death/Void entry, and it is why the Black set holds no animals: the dead are raised, not grown.

## Appendix C — Cross-Cutting Rules

A few structural rules apply across every color's roster:

- **Death is near-universal** — almost any creature can take a Death-variant reflection of itself, per Appendix B.
- **Decay and poison are modifiers**, not their own category — any creature can carry a decaying or venomous variant.
- **Wild fauna, not domesticated stock** — animals appear in ancestral/wild forms, never as modern pets or livestock.

**Golems/Constructs and Megalith Guardians** are a separate, cross-cutting archetype family tied to the world's two deep-past epochs (Ancient Civilization, Hunter-Gatherer — Book VI). Neither is a standing world feature: both enter play only via builder-deployed Story Content Packs (Book II), never as baked-in world-gen dressing. Golems are unaffected by the Black/Void constraint — nothing died to make one, so it reflects nothing. Megalith Guardians wake on celestial triggers, not on sight or sound.

## Appendix D — Entry Formats and Power Tiers

**The creature template.** Every creature entry uses this shape:

- **Name**, grouped under its color's Animals / Monsters / Terrors subheadings.
- **Environments** — biome-flavor tags within its color. **Two different registers, by rank (clarified 2026-07-04, ledger pending):** for **Animals**, this is genuine ecological terrain — the conditions Appendix E's simulation actually spawns them from. For **Monsters** and **Terrors**, this is dungeon-placement flavor, not a spawn condition — these are the game's dungeon-oriented, DM-recruited population, and the tag describes where a builder would thematically site them, not where the world grows them. A Monster or Terror *can* still turn up in the open world as an unbuilt "spawn camp" or wandering band — a possibility, not the norm — but the primary path to meeting one is a Dungeon Maker's build. Do not read a Monster/Terror's Environments tag as a claim about the ecological simulation.
- **Description** — appearance, flavor, tone.
- **Behaviors** — behavior pattern: solitary/pack, aggression, signature actions.
- **Threats & Defenses** — what it inflicts and resists, drawn from Book IV's status-effect taxonomy (Root, Snare, Slow, Expose, Poison/Disease, Stun, Silence, Confound, Sunder, Fear, Panic, Charm) — no bespoke effects per creature.
- **Statistics** — a table with Base (Normal) plus computed Weak (×0.6), Strong (×1.75), and Boss (×4, a floor — named unique Bosses embody or exceed it), across the seven player stat lanes (Str, Dex, Con, Int, Wis, Luck, End). Health/Stamina/Mana stay derived-and-`[BUILD]` per Book I.
- **Attack Patterns** — named, Souls-like specific moves: a base attack, a signature move, and where it applies, a stationary vs. in-motion variant.
- **Materials** — harvestable parts, salvage, or (for the immaterial) nothing. Not every entry yields anything of worth.
- **Weapons / Armor tables** — *conditional*, only for creatures that would plausibly wield a weapon or wear armor.

**Power tiers.** Four bands per creature, not a rival to Book II's ten Dungeon-Maker tiers:

| Tier | Scale | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weak | ~60% of Normal | young, wounded, or lesser specimens |
| Normal | baseline | the stat block given in each entry |
| Strong | ~175% of Normal | elite/alpha variant, often one extra trick |
| Boss | several multiples of Normal | unique, named instance; always ≥1 signature mechanic |

Weak/Normal are available from a dungeon's earliest tiers by default. Strong requires ranking Book II's **Monster Mastery** tech tree to mid-level — the realistic ceiling for most builds. Boss is gated by the same top-tier/deep-tech-tree wall as raid-zone and Advent-gate construction — most builds cap below it by design.

**The Inverted exception.** The Inverted set uses a different shape entirely — the **Phenomenon template** (Name / Class / Manifestation / Zone & Trigger / Effect / Source & Counter / Encounter Use), documented in that set's own opening. Its phenomena have no stat block and no power tiers; they scale by **intensity** (low → high), and they double as the psychic-effect library that boss and room construction draws from.

## Appendix E — Biome Seeding and the Ecological Simulation

Biomes are not hand-authored zones, and their creature benches are not a lookup table. The world is generated as a grid of hexes — each roughly fifty miles across (the engine's world-definition system) — and each hex's contents are procedurally generated and, in the same pass, **classified**. That classification is at once a constraint on generation and an output of it, and it feeds many downstream systems, creature spawning among them.

Biome type emerges from the underlying simulation, centrally the **water cycle** and the **compound/element life-cycle** (Book III). The ecology assembles itself in trophic layers rather than being placed: where water meets nutrients, plants can grow; a plant-growing biome draws an insect layer; the insect layer draws a small-animal layer; and so on up the chain, predators and monsters riding on the layers beneath them.

Creatures spawn from the conditions the simulation detects, not from an assigned list. The bestiary's own flavor already encodes the hooks — a vulture rides heat updrafts, so the presence of those updrafts in the water simulation is itself the key indicator that tells the procedural system to spawn vultures there. Every color roster's creatures attach to the conditions that suit them the same way, and the same detected conditions decide, per Book II, which creatures a Dungeon Maker can recruit from the biome their entrance sits in.

The nine working biome categories — Desert, Volcanic, Mountain/Highland, Underland, Temperate Forest, Tropical/Jungle, Wetland, Coastal/Ocean, Tundra/Polar — are shorthands for recurring bundles of those conditions, not places.

**[STUB]** The water cycle and the full ecological-spawn system are not yet built — engine work. The exact condition-to-creature mappings follow once that simulation exists. The world otherwise holds no fixed authored geography beyond one production-practical player-built town; anything that looks like a ruin is ordinary erosion-system output, and the Ancient Civilization / Hunter-Gatherer flavor enters play only via builder-deployed Story Content Packs.

## Appendix F — Boss Composition

A Vetala riding a Dracolich is a worked example of archetype + word-grammar composition, stacked — both halves are independent "reflections, not creation" under the Black/Void constraint. It is one candidate direction for how bosses compose generally, and it pairs with the dual-boss reanimation mechanic in the Black set (the Vetala). Not yet ruled as *the* boss-composition system.

## Appendix G — Design Notes and Open Questions

**Tone.** Traditional high fantasy with a gothic edge. The roster is built from recognizable staples — unicorns, griffins, and dragons alongside goblins, ogres, cyclopes, and the classic undead — written with atmosphere and genuine danger, but for broad appeal rather than shock. *Gothic* here means shadowed, eerie, and serious: the ominous ruin, the thing best left undisturbed, the beast that is genuinely dangerous to meet. It is horror-adjacent, not horror outright — we deliberately stopped short of the decay, corruption, and body-horror of the grimmest end of the genre. Grim where a thing earns it, never grim for its own sake, and welcoming to a reader who simply likes dragons. This governs every entry.

**Invented framing [PROPOSED].** The in-world compiler and naturalist voices of the Introduction (Corvane, Verda, Lucen, Bralt, Ossian, Virel) are newly invented as of 2026-07-04 and await Elideus's ratification before they are canon.

**Open questions:**

- **Human Principalities — structurally resolved.** The four human Principalities *are* the mortal-color domain schools (Yellow, Red, Blue, Green). Open: the placement of specific figures in the myth — the Lonely Muse was originally a student of Blue.
- **Psionics and the hidden face of Chaos — emerging (not yet canon).** The First Titan's dominant face is Death/Void (Black); a hidden Chaos (Orange) face behind it teaches a chosen few the "true nature of the universe," and Psionics is that hidden school of Chaos. Whether the teaching is truth or merely another perspective is a deliberate, unresolved theme (the forbidden route to power against the consensus). Belongs to Book VI once matured.
- **Recruitment gating — resolved (2026-07-04).** Learning is biome-gated, using is not: a Dungeon Maker first learns a creature by recruiting it from its native biome, which unlocks that creature (and its Monster Mastery tech-tree branch) permanently. A dungeon built and operated within a biome is what unlocks the tech tree for that biome's sets and features in the first place. Once learned, a creature is part of the Maker's permanent roster — the tech tree gates access generally (Weak/Normal by default, Strong/Boss by rank, per Appendix D), not biome-of-current-build. See Book II, Placement and Monster Recruitment.
- **Biome benches — resolved in direction.** Emergent from the ecological simulation, not a table (Appendix E).
